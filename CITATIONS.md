# Research Citations

Every risk factor weight in Nopilepsy is derived from published epilepsy research. This document lists all studies used.

---

## 1. Sleep Duration (Weight: 0.22)

**Malow BA, Passaro E, et al.** (2004)
*Sleep deprivation as a risk factor for seizures in idiopathic generalized epilepsy*
Neurology
DOI: [10.1212/01.WNL.0000137583.15232.84](https://doi.org/10.1212/01.WNL.0000137583.15232.84)

> 28% of patients with idiopathic generalized epilepsy reported sleep deprivation as a seizure trigger.

## 2. Sleep Quality (Weight: 0.10)

**Bazil CW** (2003)
*Sleep and epilepsy: a clinical review*
Current Neurology and Neuroscience Reports
DOI: [10.1007/s11910-003-0028-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11910-003-0028-8)

> Disrupted sleep architecture correlates with increased seizure frequency.

## 3. Heart Rate Variability (Weight: 0.20)

**Jansen K, Lagae L** (2010)
*Autonomic dysfunction in epilepsy: Heart rate variability analysis*
European Journal of Paediatric Neurology
DOI: [10.1016/j.ejpn.2010.01.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejpn.2010.01.003)

> Reduced HRV (SDNN <50ms) indicates autonomic dysfunction preceding seizure events.

## 4. Resting Heart Rate (Weight: 0.10)

**Sevcencu C, Struijk JJ** (2010)
*Ictal tachycardia as an epilepsy biomarker*
Epilepsia
DOI: [10.1111/j.1528-1167.2010.02571.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1528-1167.2010.02571.x)

> Elevated resting heart rate reflects autonomic stress state associated with seizure susceptibility.

## 5. Blood Oxygen / SpO2 (Weight: 0.08)

**Bateman LM, Li CS, Seyal M** (2008)
*Oxygen desaturation during seizures in epilepsy patients*
Neurology
DOI: [10.1212/01.wnl.0000318293.87512.a8](https://doi.org/10.1212/01.wnl.0000318293.87512.a8)

> Oxygen desaturation (<94%) was observed in 33% of seizure events.

## 6. Skin Temperature (Weight: 0.05)

**Dubey D, et al.** (2005)
*Fever as a seizure precipitant in epilepsy patients*
Epilepsia
DOI: [10.1111/j.1528-1167.2005.00251.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1528-1167.2005.00251.x)

> Elevated body temperature (>0.5C above baseline) acts as a seizure trigger.

## 7. Activity Level (Weight: 0.05)

**Nakken KO** (1999)
*Physical exercise and epilepsy*
Epilepsia
DOI: [10.1111/j.1528-1157.1999.tb00721.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1528-1157.1999.tb00721.x)

> Physical exhaustion reported as a seizure trigger by ~10% of epilepsy patients.

## 8. Medication Adherence (Weight: 0.15)

**Faught E, Duh MS, Weiner JR, et al.** (2008)
*Adherence to antiepileptic drugs and seizure risk*
Neurology
DOI: [10.1212/01.wnl.0000324954.49966.32](https://doi.org/10.1212/01.wnl.0000324954.49966.32)

> Missed AED doses increased seizure risk by 21-fold within 2 days.

## 9. Stress (Weight: 0.05)

**Neufeld MY, Sadeh M, Cohn DF, et al.** (1994)
*Stress and epilepsy: patient perception and objective assessment*
Epilepsia
DOI: [10.1111/j.1528-1157.1994.tb02480.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1528-1157.1994.tb02480.x)

> Stress self-reported as a seizure trigger by 30% of epilepsy patients.
